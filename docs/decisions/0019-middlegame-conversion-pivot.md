# 0019 · Middlegame conversion focus and a validation-first plan (2026-11-07)

Status: accepted by the owner in chat (2026-11-07). Amends ADR 0017 (generated drills) and the positioning in `PRODUCT.md` and `docs/spec.md` v0.4 (audience, headline outcome, surface order).

## Why

The project grew into many features. The owner's original aim is narrower: opening, endgame and general trainers exist, but almost none focus on the **middlegame and tactics**, i.e. how to turn a good opening into a win. The owner's own data supports it: of 541 analysed Chess.com games, 227 (42%) were +1.0 or better for the owner after move 10; 149 were won and 71 lost (depth-10 pass, one player, rough).

## Decisions

1. **Audience:** online players rated 500 to 1600, rapid (10 minutes or more). The owner (about 570) is the first user.
2. **Headline outcome:** conversion rate (see `GLOSSARY.md`): games won out of games with a good opening, a good opening being at least +0.5 for the player at the checkpoint position, after Black's 5th move (amended 2026-11-08; first written as +1.0 after move 10). A +1.0 version is shown beside it. Mistake rates (M1, M2) stay as the diagnosis of why advantages were squandered. Replaces "mistake rates" as the headline in `PRODUCT.md`.
3. **Teaching layers:** Layer 1 is verified tactics (hanging pieces, free pieces). Layer 2 is conversion habits (simplify when ahead, trade down, do not rush); it is a later hypothesis, not in the first test, because no detector can verify it.
4. **Two doors:** Door 1, anyone starts at once without an account (public drills, a username for a report); Door 2, players who connect their games get a personal report and own drills.
5. **Validate before building the product.** The first claim is demand and return visits, not rating gain. Test product: enter a Chess.com username, get a **conversion report** for the last 20 rapid games (headline, up to three squandered advantages with a verified reason only where a detector supports it, five drills ordered own drills, then public, then generated), plus play against Stockfish or Maia and a convert-this-advantage game. No sign-in, no saved history.
6. **Surfaces:** a basic one-page website, not every tester has ChatGPT or Claude, and the existing Claude connector (ChatGPT smoke test before recruiting), both thin fronts on the same service. Chess.com rapid only for the test; Lichess after.
7. **Report is not instant.** A shallow depth-10 pass finds the opening exit eval; the deeper depth-18 check runs only on turning points. The page lets the person leave and return. Real timings are measured before anything is promised.
8. **Pre-registered bar, written before recruiting:** 15 testers over 2 weeks, after 5 friends find bugs. **Go:** at least 8 finish the report and at least 5 come back for a second session unprompted. **Change course:** fewer than 3 come back, or most say it is interesting but they would not train. Testers are strangers from chess communities, not only friends.
9. **Generated drills (amends ADR 0017):** positions may be generated by letting Maia play on from real opening exit positions at the player's band, with Stockfish choosing the verified answer. Stockfish playing both sides is rejected: it never reaches the loose-piece positions 500 to 1600 players meet. Generated drills are labelled, ranked after real-game drills, and enter the library only after passing the Yes / No / Can't tell hand-check (gate 18 of 20). Maia is offered only for rating bands it supports; Stockfish skill levels below that (check the supported bands in code first).
10. **Existing work is audited, not deleted** (keep / park / cut table in `docs/product-spec-v1.md`; the owner decides each row).

## Evidence check on the owner's games (2026-11-07, after a first-principles review)

Data: 627 Chess.com games synced 2026-11-07, 584 usable (43 ended before move 11); stored depth-10 pass for the position after move 10; detectors `hanging_own` v4 (M1) and `missed_free` v3 (M2) at the pinned depth 18. One player (about 570); scratch scripts, not in the repo.

| Position after move 10 | Games | Won | Drew | Lost | Share of the 269 losses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ahead (+1.0 or better) | 236 | 154 | 7 | 75 | 28% |
| Level | 154 | 70 | 12 | 72 | 27% |
| Behind (-1.0 or worse) | 194 | 60 | 12 | 122 | 45% |

Squandered advantages are the smaller leak for the owner. The 47 newest games alone are too few to show a trend.

Detectors on the 76 games lost from an ahead position (turning point = the first move taking the lead from +1.0 or better to below +0.3, median swing about +4.3 to -2.7 at ply 28): hung own piece 18 (24%), missed free piece 1, looked and found no simple tactic 5, abstained 26 (34%), skipped as "decided" 24 (32%, the detectors ignore positions better than +6.0), lead never fell below +0.3 for 2. Where a verdict exists, 19 of 24 are hung or missed pieces. 66% of cases get no verdict.

| # | Hypothesis | Status |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Almost nobody focuses on the middlegame and tactics | **Refuted for conversion and for tactics from your own games** (Aimchess has a conversion replay; several tools build puzzles from your mistakes; conversion courses are common). See `docs/competitor-scan-2026-11-07.md`. The unexplored angle is measured proof of improvement, which the scan did not find |
| H2 | Conversion is a big leak | Weak for the owner: 28% of losses; 45% are lost from a bad position |
| H3 | Players want a report of their own games | Untested; the validation test |
| H4 | Squandered advantages are tactical errors the detectors can see | Partly supported by our data: where there is a verdict it is mostly a hung piece, but about a third of cases get one. **Contested elsewhere** (`docs/competitive-analysis-2026-11-08.md`): player threads and a coach's newsletter disagree about whether tactics are the cause |
| H5 | Strangers return without an account | Untested; the validation test |

**Proposed, not yet decided by the owner:**
- Reframe the test product as a **loss map** (each loss sorted by ahead / level / behind after move 10 plus its first decisive error), with conversion as one of three zones, instead of a conversion-only report. The engine work is nearly the same; it avoids telling a tester their problem is conversion when it is their openings.
- Promise a verified reason only where a detector has a verdict. A detector change (for example the "decided" limit of 600 cp) needs its own version, ADR and hand-check.
- Rename "good opening" to a label that does not claim opening skill (for example "lead after move 10"): a +1.0 lead is often the opponent's early slip.

## Amendment 2026-11-08: measure from move 5, not move 10 (owner)

The product's opening ends after Black's 4th move and it measures and trains from move 5 (see `GLOSSARY.md`). Reasons from the owner's data: the detectors first look at ply 8 (White's 5th move, `prd_rules.MIN_PLY`), and 41% of the games that end up behind after move 10 went wrong by move 4 and 68% by move 6, so a move-10 boundary hides most of the first slips. The term "opening exit position" now means the position after move 4. The evidence tables above stay as measured at move 10; they are history, not the new definition.

Effect on the zone split at the earlier checkpoint (same 618 games, depth-10 pass):

| Checkpoint | Threshold | Ahead | Level | Behind | Share of games sorted (ahead or behind) | Share of losses that start level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| After move 4 | ±1.0 | 105 | 410 | 104 | 34% | 68% |
| After move 4 | ±0.5 | 179 | 276 | 164 | 55% | 44% |
| After move 5 | ±1.0 | 138 | 343 | 137 | 44% | 57% |
| After move 5 | ±0.5 | 196 | 224 | 198 | 64% | 41% |
| After move 10 (before) | ±1.0 | 241 | 154 | 197 | 66% | 26% |

At move 4 or 5 most games are still level at ±1.0, so "good opening" and a conversion rate sorted at that checkpoint cover few games and mean something different ("a lead from early play, often the opponent's slip"). **Decided by the owner (2026-11-08): the zone split is taken at the checkpoint position after Black's 5th move, with a ±0.5 threshold.** On the 618 games (depth-10 pass, one player): ahead 196 (117 won, 9 drawn, 70 lost), level 224 (99 won, 114 lost), behind 198 (90 won, 97 lost). That gives a conversion rate of 117 out of 196 (60%), and the losses split 25% ahead, 41% level, 35% behind. The move-10 tables above are history. Caveats: a ±0.5 lead after 5 moves is often the opponent's slip, not good opening play, and depth-10 evaluations this early are noisy (154 of 351 games that dipped to -1.0 by move 10 were back above it a move later); the numbers were measured once on the games used to choose the checkpoint, so a later window is the real test. Any new definition of "good opening" or of a detector's first move is a new version and a re-measure, not a retune on these games.

## What this does not show

- No claim that the product raises ratings. Conversion rate across months and the 72-hour delayed test are the later evidence.
- The 42% / 66% figures come from one player's games at depth 10.
- Detectors were hand-checked mainly on games up to about 1200.

## Reversal

Docs only. Reverting this ADR restores the earlier "own mistakes" positioning; no code or data is changed by it.
